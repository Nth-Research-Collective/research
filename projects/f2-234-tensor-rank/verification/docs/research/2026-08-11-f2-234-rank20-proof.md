## Theorem

Let \(M_{2,3,4}\) be the tensor of multiplying a \(2\times3\) matrix by a
\(3\times4\) matrix over \(\mathbb F_2\). Then

\[
R_{\mathbb F_2}(M_{2,3,4})=20.
\]

The rank-20 upper bound is the exact AlphaTensor decomposition already replayed
against all 576 coefficients. The new work is the exclusion of rank 19.

## 1. Row restrictions

Write

\[
A=\mathbb F_2^2,\qquad B=\mathbb F_2^3,\qquad C=\mathbb F_2^4,
\]

and identify the three tensor-factor coordinate spaces with
\(A\otimes B\), \(B\otimes C\), and \(A\otimes C\). Suppose

\[
M_{2,3,4}=\sum_{s=1}^{19}U_s\otimes V_s\otimes W_s
\tag{1}
\]

with all three factors of every summand nonzero.

For nonzero \(x\in A\), restrict the first matrix to matrices
\(X=xy^{\mathsf T}\), \(y\in B\). Define

\[
H_x=x\otimes C\subseteq A\otimes C,
\qquad
R_x=B\otimes H_x.
\]

The restricted target is

\[
T_x=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{k=1}^{4}
e_j\otimes E_{jk}\otimes(x\otimes f_k).
\tag{2}
\]

Its flattening with \(B\otimes C\) on one side has rank 12 and image
exactly \(R_x\), which also has dimension 12. For distinct nonzero
\(x,y\in A\),

\[
H_x\cap H_y=\{0\}.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(U_s=a_s b_s^{\mathsf T}\) has rank one, its restriction is active
exactly when \(a_s\mathbin\cdot x=1\), and its other flattening factor is

\[
q_{s,x}=b_s\otimes W_s.
\tag{4}
\]

If \(U_*\) has rank two, it is active for every nonzero \(x\), because
\(x^{\mathsf T}U_*\ne0\), and

\[
q_{*,x}=(x^{\mathsf T}U_*)\otimes W_*.
\tag{5}
\]

## 2. Two flattening lemmas

### Lemma 1: tight 12-term restriction

If \(T_x\) is expressed by exactly 12 active rank-one terms, then the 12
vectors \(V_s\) are a basis of \(B\otimes C\), and the 12 vectors
\(q_{s,x}\) are a basis of \(R_x\). In particular, every active rank-one
term satisfies \(W_s\in H_x\).

**Proof.** A rank-12 matrix written as a sum of 12 dyads forces both factor
lists to have rank 12. Hence the \(q_{s,x}\) span the flattening image
\(R_x\), so each lies in \(R_x\). If \(b_s\otimes W_s\in B\otimes H_x\)
and \(b_s\ne0\), apply a functional taking \(b_s\) to 1 to conclude
\(W_s\in H_x\). \(\square\)

### Lemma 2: one-excess 13-term restriction

Suppose \(T_x\) is expressed by 13 active terms. Let

\[
L_x:\mathbb F_2^{13}\longrightarrow B\otimes C,
\qquad e_s\longmapsto V_s.
\]

Then \(L_x\) is surjective and has a one-dimensional kernel
\(\langle\lambda^x\rangle\). If \(\pi_x\) is the quotient map onto
\((B\otimes(A\otimes C))/R_x\), there is one quotient vector \(z_x\) such
that

\[
\pi_x(q_{s,x})=\lambda^x_s z_x
\quad\text{for all active }s.
\tag{6}
\]

**Proof.** Flattening rank 12 implies that the active \(V_s\) span at least
12 dimensions; their ambient space has dimension 12. Thus \(L_x\) is
surjective and its kernel has dimension one. Projecting the target to the
quotient gives

\[
\sum_s V_s\otimes\pi_x(q_{s,x})=0.
\]

This tensor lies in
\(\ker(L_x)\otimes((B\otimes(A\otimes C))/R_x)\), which gives (6).
\(\square\)

We also use the elementary fact that over \(\mathbb F_2\), equality of two
nonzero pure tensors \(b\otimes h=b'\otimes h'\) forces
\(b=b'\) and \(h=h'\). Over a general field the factors are proportional;
the only nonzero scalar in \(\mathbb F_2\) is 1.

## 4. Excluding \((7,7,5)\)

There are two row directions occurring seven times. Restricting against the
annihilator of either direction leaves exactly \(19-7=12\) terms. By Lemma 1,
all active \(W_s\) lie in the corresponding \(H_x\).

The two active sets overlap in the five terms from the deficient row direction.
Their W factors lie in \(H_x\cap H_y=\{0\}\), contradicting nonzero factors.
Thus orbit 0 is impossible.

## 5. Excluding \((7,6,6)\)

Let \(a_0\) be the multiplicity-seven direction and \(a_1,a_2\) the two
multiplicity-six directions. Restriction against the annihilator \(x_0\) of
\(a_0\) leaves the 12 terms in groups \(a_1,a_2\). Lemma 1 forces all their
W factors into \(H_{x_0}\).

Now restrict against the annihilator \(x_1\) of \(a_1\). The active terms are
the seven from \(a_0\) and the six from \(a_2\), hence 13 terms. Lemma 2
applies. For each of the six \(a_2\) terms, its nonzero W factor is in
\(H_{x_0}\), and projection modulo \(H_{x_1}\) is injective on
\(H_{x_0}\). Therefore its quotient pure tensor is nonzero. Equation (6)
forces all six relevant \(\lambda_s\) to equal 1 and all six quotient pure
tensors to equal the same nonzero \(z_{x_1}\).

Within one fixed row direction, distinct U factors have distinct nonzero
\(b_s\). Equality of those six nonzero pure tensors is therefore impossible.
Thus orbits 1 and 2 are impossible.

## 6. Excluding the one-rank-two orbit

There are six rank-one terms in each row direction and one rank-two term.
Every nonzero row restriction has 12 active rank-one terms plus the rank-two
term, so Lemma 2 applies to all three restrictions.

First, \(z_x\ne0\) for every nonzero \(x\). If \(z_x=0\), equation (6)
puts every active W factor in \(H_x\). Choose \(y\ne x\). The two active sets
overlap in one complete six-term rank-one row group and the rank-two term.
All seven overlap W factors are nonzero and lie in \(H_x\), hence none lies in
\(H_y\). Their quotient tensors for restriction \(y\) are therefore nonzero,
so all seven relation coefficients are 1 and all seven quotient tensors equal
the same nonzero \(z_y\). The six rank-one terms have distinct \(b_s\), which
contradicts uniqueness of nonzero pure-tensor factorization.

Consequently every \(z_x\) is nonzero. For a fixed restriction, every term in
the support of \(\lambda^x\) has the same nonzero quotient pure tensor. There
can be at most one such term from each of the two active rank-one row groups,
because the \(b_s\) within a group are distinct, plus the one rank-two term.
Hence

\[
|\operatorname{supp}(\lambda^x)|\le3.
\tag{7}
\]

Each of the 18 rank-one terms is active in exactly two of the three row
restrictions. If its relation coefficient were zero in both, equation (6)
would put its W factor in two distinct spaces \(H_x,H_y\), forcing \(W_s=0\).
Thus every rank-one term occurs in at least one relation support. This requires
at least 18 support incidences. But (7) permits at most

\[
3\text{ restrictions}\times3\text{ terms}=9
\]

incidences, a contradiction. Orbit 3 is impossible.
